### Probe selection: one probe per gene

Default since issue #10: `select_probes(method="max_mean")` keeps, for each gene, the probe with the **highest mean expression across all 438 samples**. It is used by `load_clock_genes()` and `collapse_probes_to_genes()`. Averaging probes (`collapse="mean"`) is still available. Averaging would mix near-background probes into the signal (PER3 17478: mean log2 3.86, minimum 1.21). The rule ignores time, rhythmicity and genotype, so it cannot bias the rhythm or genotype tests. ## 8. Timing anomalies (for Part 2; not modified)

Checks per subject × condition: hours_awake = 7.5 + 3·(T−1); (clock_time − circadian_phase) mod 24 constant within ±1 h; clock-time gap between consecutive samples ≈ 3 h per timepoint step (±0.5 h). Saved to `results/tables/sample_anomalies.csv`.

| sample_id | subject | condition | timepoint | clock_time | circadian_phase_h | hours_awake | issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GSM969076 | AF0182 | SE | T4 | 2:25 | -3.0 | 16.5 | clock_time - circadian_phase differs from series median by -2.00 h; clock-time gap from previous sample 4.00 h (expected ~3 h) |
| GSM969077 | AF0182 | SE | T5 | 4:25 | -3.0 | 19.5 | clock-time gap from previous sample 2.00 h (expected ~3 h) |
| GSM969096 | AF0186 | SE | T8 | 14:12 | 9.0 | 28.5 | clock-time gap from previous sample 3.70 h (expected ~3 h) |
| GSM969231 | AF0318 | SE | T10 | 18:10 | 15.0 | 34.5 | clock-time gap from previous sample 3.67 h (expected ~3 h) |

`circadian_phase_h` takes only the values -15, -12, -9, -6, -3, 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15 (3-h steps). It is a binned assignment, not a continuous per-sample measurement.

Implied clock time of circadian phase 0 (`clock_time_h − circadian_phase_h` mod 24, per-subject median): SE: mean 04:10 (range 02:15–07:25); SR: mean 05:08 (range 01:15–08:10). Möller-Levet et al. (2013) report mean plasma-melatonin **midpoints** of 04:15 (control) and 05:01 (sleep restriction), consistent with phase 0 = melatonin midpoint (inference; the SI Methods definition was not checked).
